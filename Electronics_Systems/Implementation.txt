Why common GND is correct (not a problem)
Voltage is always measured relative to something. "5V" means 5V above ground. If your ESP32 and your buck converter don't share the same ground reference, then "5V" on one board isn't the same "5V" the other board sees — the numbers become meaningless and signals/power don't work.

Battery 12V ─┬─────────────── 12V rail ── motors / BTS7960 B+
             │
          Buck#1 ── 5V rail ── ESP32 VIN, Nova PM, servos
             │
          Buck#2 ── 3.3V rail ── SCD4X, VEML7700
             │
   ALL negatives tie together ── ONE common GND ──┘
